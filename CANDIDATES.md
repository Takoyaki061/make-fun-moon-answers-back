# Candidates — The Moon Finally Answers Back

Judge brief (in order): 1) clearly the Moon speaking back to wishers / howlers / late-night promisers; 2) funny on one read, a real laugh; 3) wry, old, a little tired of being wished upon; 4) comeback turn lands before the line ends; 5) no wasted characters. A line that explains its own joke has already lost.

## Critique (DeepSeek, second call — TOP3: H3, M4, H1)

- **H3** — "Only one that's unmistakably the Moon fending off howlers, and 'avoiding Tuesday' turns the comeback with zero fat where M4 leans on a dating-text punchline and H1 never actually turns back on the wisher."
- **M4** — "Two beats, brutal turn, and the reversal ('I showed up') is cleaner and shorter than H1's one-note observation, which is funny but never swings back."
- **H1** — "Tightest of the rest and genuinely funny on one read, but it's a complaint, not a comeback — H3 and M4 both land a turn, H1 just lands."
- **WORST3:** W2 (soggy, two-clause sprawl), M3 (vending-machine cliché), H4 (alibi muddles premise).

## My own verdicts (against the brief, in my words)

- **H3 — PICK.** Wins criterion 1 outright: only the Moon is "still up" every night, and "I'm working" is the Moon's own job description. The turn ("you're just avoiding Tuesday") arrives late in the line and flips shared-night solidarity into an accusation; "Tuesday" is the funniest weekday to avoid — dull, specific, not worth staying up for. Zero fat at 110 chars, explains nothing.
- **M4 — runner-up.** Cleanest two-beat reversal in the pool ("I showed up"), and it answers a late-night promise head-on. Loses to H3 on criteria 1 and 3: it could be said by an ex, a friend, anyone — it is not the Moon's voice.
- **H1 — third.** Genuinely funny on one read and the tightest observation, but it is a complaint, not a comeback: it never swings back at the howler.
- **H2 — fourth.** The tide keeping a schedule is a lovely image, but the line is about the tide, not the Moon answering; the Moon is a bystander in its own line.
- **W1 — fifth.** "I'm a rock, not a bookie" is a good punch, but the setup is long and the report framing ("I'm here to report") spends characters before the joke starts.
- **W4 — sixth.** "Technically you're all in debt to me" is a strong closer, but 164 chars of setup for one payoff, and it answers only lottery wishers.
- **M2 — seventh.** "Mind my own craters" is a nice Moon-voice phrase, but the first clause ("like I run the place") is a shrug, not a joke; the turn is weak.
- **W3 — eighth.** Clever ("take the money instead") but the fame premise is convoluted: being stared at then looked away from needs a second read to land.
- **M1 — ninth.** Sweet and Moon-voiced, but it is a sigh, not a comeback — no turn, no target.
- **H4 — tenth.** "Tomorrow's alibi" is a good phrase, but the premise muddles: the Moon as alibi for procrastination doesn't quite parse on one read.
- **M3 — eleventh.** "Vending machine" is a cliché the judge has seen a thousand times; the line is a bumper sticker.
- **W2 — twelfth.** Two clauses that never meet: the loneliness setup and the "which one of us should be wishing" payoff argue past each other.

## Pick

**H3** — `Yes, I'm still up. No, that doesn't make us "in this together" — I'm working; you're just avoiding Tuesday.` (107 characters, 1 line, UTF-8, verified by running main.py)
